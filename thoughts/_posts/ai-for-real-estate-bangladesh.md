Ask a Bangladeshi property buyer what they want and they will say price, location, size. Listen longer and you hear something else: **"Ei jomir kagoj ki clean? Ashol dam koto?"** — is this land's paperwork clean, and what is the real price? I learned this working on Proyojon Abashon, and it changed how I think about AI in real estate.

## What do property buyers actually fear?

When I mapped what buyers said, meant, feared and shared, the pattern was clear:

- **They say:** "Is this land's paperwork clean? What's the real price?"
- **They mean:** "I can't afford one wrong move with my family's savings."
- **They fear:** fake deeds, hidden DAP problems, and being rushed by a broker.
- **They share:** anything that protects their family and makes them look careful.

So the first problem in real estate is not lead generation. It is **trust**. Any AI that ignores that will just produce faster, pushier sales messages — exactly what buyers are afraid of.

## What we did at Proyojon Abashon

Proyojon Abashon is a land-share and property management platform in Dhaka. Instead of starting with offers, we started with protection: a calm buyer-education series called **ক্রেতা সুরক্ষা** (buyer protection) that explains deeds, DAP and home loans *before* ever asking for a sale. For **Proyojon Green Vista**, a G+9 lake-view land-share development, I worked on land-share visualization and a buyer-facing structure so people could see what they were buying into.

That is the method from [Content Starts in the Audience's Head](../content-starts-in-the-audiences-head/): listen, decode the real need, then frame everything around it.

## Where does AI fit in real estate?

AI is useful exactly where the same trust questions repeat every day:

1. **Answering document questions.** "Namjari ki?", "DAP e ki dekhte hobe?", "home loan e koto down payment lage?" — an AI assistant trained on your own explanations can answer these in Bangla, Banglish and English at any hour, and link to the right explainer.
2. **Sorting enquiries.** It can ask a few calm questions — budget, area, family size, timeline, loan or cash — and mark who is ready for a visit and who is still researching. Your team calls the ready ones first.
3. **Preparing site visits.** Before a meeting, it collects what the buyer already has and lists what they still need, so the visit is about the property, not paperwork.
4. **Drafting buyer education.** AI can turn your team's real answers into first drafts of posts, short videos and FAQs, which a person checks and publishes.

The same intake pattern — *problem → context → documents*, then *type → urgency → required files* — is what I designed for Proyojon Solutions' advisory work, and it fits property enquiries well.

## What AI should never do in real estate

- **Declare a deed or title clean.** It can explain which documents to ask for; a lawyer or qualified person checks the originals.
- **Promise a price, discount or handover date.**
- **Pressure.** No countdowns, no fake scarcity. In this market, pressure reads as a red flag.

A person makes every decision involving money, documents or legal status. AI prepares; people decide.
