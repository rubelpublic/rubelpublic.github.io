What can AI automate in your business?

A business is full of small jobs that repeat every day. These are the ones I usually start with:

  • Customer questions. An agent that answers price, size, delivery and availability questions in the customer's own language — "dam koto?", "Dhaka te delivery kobe?" — and passes anything unusual to your team.
  • Enquiry intake. The system gathers the customer's problem, context and documents, classifies the request by type and urgency, and lists what's missing before a person picks it up.
  • Content drafts. AI drafts several caption or post options in the right language and brand voice; a person picks and polishes the best one.
  • Finding information. Ask for a file or a record the way you'd ask a colleague, in any of the three languages.

How does a project run?

  1. Map one workflow on paper — trigger, understand, decide, act, check.
  2. Build a small working version and test it with real messages from your business, not invented ones.
  3. Run it alongside your team before it handles anything on its own.
  4. Pick the next workflow once the first one is reliable.

What I won't automate

Anything where a wrong answer is expensive and nobody is watching: pricing promises, legal advice, refunds, a customer's money or documents. AI can prepare that work; a person makes the final call.

Quick answers

What is AI workflow automation?

AI workflow automation means designing a system where AI handles a repeatable business task from start to finish, such as reading incoming messages, sorting them, drafting replies and updating records, while a person checks the decisions that matter.

Can the AI understand and reply in Banglish?

Yes. Every agent Rapar Rubel builds is designed and tested with real customer messages in Bangla, Banglish and English, including spelling variations, and replies in the language the customer used.

What is a good first workflow to automate?

Pick a task that is repetitive, happens many times a week and has a clear right answer. Answering your 20 most common customer questions, or sorting incoming enquiries by type and urgency, are typical first workflows.

What should a business not automate?

Anything where a wrong answer is expensive and nobody is watching: pricing promises, legal advice, refunds, or anything involving a customer's money or documents. AI can prepare that work, but a person should make the final call.
